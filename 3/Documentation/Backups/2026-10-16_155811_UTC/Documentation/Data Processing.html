Where the saved sample starts and ends.

01Storage

Percent full is the level the curve trades against. Working-gas volume has risen from about 618 TWh to about 1,132 TWh, so a gap in TWh is a different object at the two ends of the sample.

Input1. Raw Data/funda.csv
Span1 Jan 2011 – 8 Oct 2026 · 5,760 gas days
Lagshift(2) after the features
  1. Drop consumption, consumptionFull and trend. Consumption sits near a constant, including in January 2011 and October 2026, so it is not daily demand.
  2. Set agsi_estimated = 1 when status is E. That is 2,984 days, including 7–8 Oct 2026. Estimated days stay in the sample.
  3. Check full against gasInStorage / workingGasVolume × 100. The two agree within 0.005 percentage points. Keep the published full.
  4. full_norm5y is the mean of full on the same calendar day in the five preceding years only. Map a 29 February lookup to 28 February, and keep 29 February out of the 28 February average. storage_dev is the difference, in percentage points. Square it for storage_dev_sq.
  5. net_inj = −netWithdrawal / 1000, in TWh/d, so injection is positive. inj_util and wd_util divide each flow by its capacity. Capacities are never zero.
  6. Apply shift(2) on the gas-day index. A revised AGSI stock is a little kinder than the number a trader saw that evening; say so when the backtest is written.

Dev = Full − mean of the same day in years t−5 … t−1

The normal uses past years only, so Dev starts on 1 Jan 2016. The weather regression for the flow is not built in this file.

Send-out rose after 2022 because Europe built import capacity. The baseline is the past year. There is no inventory column in this file.

03Realised weather

Temperature is not stationary. The normal is the same calendar day over the previous ten years, excluding the year itself. That is the rule already used for fc_hdd_anom7.

Input1. Raw Data/weather_hist.csv
Span1 Jan 1981 – 6 Oct 2026 · 16,715 valid days
Lagshift(1) after the sums
  1. Drop the six blank rows from 7 Oct to 12 Oct 2026. They sit after the last ERA5 day.
  2. Rename EU_HDD and EU_CDD to hdd and cdd. Degree-days are already computed per city at 18°C and averaged with Eurostat 2023 bcm weights. The weights sum to 295.36.
  3. Subtract the mean of that calendar day over the previous ten years. Map 29 February to 28 February, as in storage. The year of day t is excluded.
  4. hdd_anom7 and cdd_anom7 sum the daily anomalies over day t and the six preceding days.
  5. Apply shift(1). Keep the history before 2016: the normal needs it. The forecast column is already built and is not recomputed here.

HDD_anom = HDD − mean of that calendar day over the previous 10 years

HDD_anom7 = Σk=0…6 HDD_anomt−k

The same two lines apply to CDD. The anomaly is a same-day object. A rolling mean of the HDD level would mix January with July.

04Prices

Settlements stay on day t. The signal is dated t and the trade is t+1, which is a backtest rule, so nothing here is shifted. S < 0 is contango and S > 0 is backwardation. The model uses the percent spread, because two euros was noise near €300 and a real gap near €20.

Input1. Raw Data/prices.csv
Span31 Mar 2011 – 12 Oct 2026 · 4,053 sessions
LagNone. P&L starts at t+1.
  1. Forward-fill the 44 blank Coal_M1 sessions with a limit of three. Each blank is one holiday, and no two are consecutive. The 2017–2019 quotes are already in the file, about $73–95/t in 2017, and stay as they are. EUR/USD is complete. Leave the 48 blank EUA_M1 sessions empty; the switch is empty there too.
  2. Keep a session only when TTF_M1, TTF_M2 and TTF_M3 all print. Fifty-one sessions have all three empty, and three have a missing leg.
  3. The euro spreads already match the differences of the settlements. Rename them s12, s13, s23, and the settlements m1, m2, m3. Percent spreads divide by the deferred month.
  4. Leave the contract-code columns blank. roll_day = 1 on the last trading day of the front month, two business days before the first calendar day of the delivery month. dte counts TTF sessions to that day. d_feb … d_dec mark the delivery month of M1, with January omitted. Through most of September that month is October, so the dummy follows the contract, not the calendar date of the trade.
  5. A log return uses the same contract on both days, so the return on roll_day is empty. Volatility is √252 times the standard deviation of the last 20 returns. sd_ds12 and sd_ds13 are the 20-day standard deviation of the daily spread change, excluding a change that crosses a roll.
  6. ds12_fwd5 and ds13_fwd5 are the spread five sessions ahead minus the spread today. Leave them empty when a roll falls inside the window. They are labels, not features.
  7. After the coal fill, convert API2 coal to €/MWh thermal and build the switch. switch_gap = m1 − ttf_switch. A positive gap means gas is the more expensive fuel in power.
  8. inj_season = 1 when the trading date is in April through September. crisis = 1 from 1 Jul 2021 through 31 Mar 2023.

S%12 = (M1 − M2) / M2 × 100

Coal €/MWh = API2$/t / EURUSD / 6.978

ttf_switch = (0.52 / 0.38) · (Coal + 0.341 · EUA) − 0.202 · EUA

6.978 MWh per tonne is 6,000 kcal/kg API2 coal. Emission factors are 0.202 for gas and 0.341 for hard coal, in tCO₂ per MWh thermal. Efficiencies are 52% for a CCGT and 38% for hard coal. API2 at $100/t, EUR/USD at 1.08 and EUA at €75 gives a switch near €38/MWh.

05Date join

Two joins, in this order. First put unshifted storage and unshifted realised weather on the same gas day, and build the surprise there. Then align every lagged series to the TTF calendar.

Storage surprise

A cold day is supposed to withdraw gas. The surprise is the flow that remains after weather and the stock level are accounted for. At each gas day, fit only on earlier days: net_inj on that day’s heating anomaly, yesterday’s storage_dev, and calendar-month dummies. surprise7 sums net_inj minus that expectation over the day and the six preceding gas days. A positive value means more gas went into storage than the weather and the stock implied, which is bearish for the front month. surprise7_z is the z-score of surprise7 against its own previous 365 days. Then shift(2), because the flow is an AGSI number.

Surprise7 = Σk=0…6 (NetInj − E[NetInj])t−k

The saved surprise is this regression. A five-year average of the flow on the same calendar day is not written to the file. Run the regression on the gas-day table. On the later TTF row, storage is already gas day t−2 and weather is gas day t−1, so those two columns are the wrong pair.

Onto the trading calendar

  1. The master index is every session from 4 Jan 2016 through 7 Oct 2026 on which all three TTF settlements exist.
  2. Bring lagged storage, lagged LNG and lagged realised weather onto that index with a backward as-of merge, tolerance of three calendar days. A Friday print that shift(2) lands on Sunday then sticks to Monday. A print that is not out yet has no row to stick.
  3. Left-join fc_hdd_anom7 from weather_forecast.csv on the issue date. The file covers 15 Feb 2024 to 5 Oct 2026, 964 days. Use shift(1) only if the forecast must be kept out of the same day’s settlement. Either way, the column is empty before 15 Feb 2024.
  4. Sort by date. One trading day, one row. Every fundamental column already carries its lag.
