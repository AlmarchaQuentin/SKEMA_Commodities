Question 3 · Dutch TTF · 10 Oct 2026

Model the front-month spreads with storage, then trade the gap.

M1 is the front-month contract, M2 the second month, M3 the third. M1/M2 and M1/M3 are those contracts against each other, not spot gas against a future. European storage is what ties the front month to the months behind it. When stocks sit above the seasonal normal the front month loses its scarcity premium and the curve is in contango, capped by the cost of storing gas. When stocks are short the front month has to ration demand and the curve goes into backwardation, with no cap. The trade is the distance between that fair value and the quoted spread, and the few days after a storage number surprises the market.

The shape you have to show

This is the theory of storage (Kaldor 1939, Working 1949). Plot it with your own data before any regression. If the cloud is flat, the rest of the project has nothing to trade.

Carry cap:   M2 − M1 ≤ M1 · r · Δt + c_storage + c_inject
If M2 traded above M1 by more than the cost of holding gas, a storage operator would buy the front month, inject, and sell M2. That arbitrage stops contango. Nothing stops backwardation: gas that is not in the ground cannot be withdrawn. With S defined as M1 − M2, contango is S < 0 and the floor on S is minus the carry cost.

Storage

Fullt = gasInStoraget / workingGasVolumet × 100    (check it against the full column)
Normt = (1/5) · Σy=1..5 Full(same calendar day, year − y)
Devt = Fullt − Normt    percentage points
Use percent full, not TWh. Working gas volume has roughly doubled since 2011 (about 618 TWh then, 1,132 TWh now), so a TWh deviation is not the same object in the two regimes. The 5-year norm uses past years only, so Dev starts in 2016. Drop 29 February from the seasonal average, or map it to 28 February. The model sample starts on the first date Dev exists.
NetInjt = − netWithdrawalt / 1000    TWh/d, positive means injecting
InjUtilt = injection / injectionCapacity    WdUtilt = withdrawal / withdrawalCapacity
Surprise7t = Σk=0..6 ( NetInjt−k − E[NetInjt−k] )
Two versions of the expected flow. v1 is the 5-year average on the same calendar day. v2 regresses the flow on the heating anomaly, yesterday’s storage deviation and month dummies, fitted only on past data. v2 is the one to keep: a withdrawal on a cold day is not news. A positive surprise means more gas went into storage than the weather and the stock level implied. That is bearish for the front month and pushes M1 down versus M2. The 7-day sum takes out end-of-storage-year noise.

LNG

If sendOut > 5 × median of the previous 30 days, replace it with that median and set sendout_bad = 1.
LNG7t = Σk=0..6 sendOutt−k / 1000    LNG_devt = LNG7t − mean(LNG7 over the previous 365 days)
A blank cell is not neutral: the 7-day sum would treat the day as zero send-out. The median is the level of the surrounding days, and sendout_bad says the figure is not the API’s. Send-out rose after 2022 because Europe built import capacity, so the deviation is from the last year, not from a 5-year normal.

Weather

weather_hist.csv already applies this. HDD = max(0, 18 − T) and CDD = max(0, T − 18) on each city, then a bcm-weighted mean. The file runs from 1981-01-01. Temperature is not stationary, so the normal is a trailing 10-year average of the same calendar day, the same rule already used in fc_hdd_anom7. A fixed 1991–2020 climate normal stays too cold.

HDDt = Σi wi · HDDi,t / Σi wi    same for CDD. Weights sum to 295.36 bcm.
HDD_anomt = HDDt − mean of HDD on that calendar day over the previous 10 years
HDD_anom7t = Σk=0..6 HDD_anomt−k
The year of day t is excluded. Map 29 February to 28 February. Do not mix seasons in a rolling mean of the HDD level. ERA5 stops on 6 Oct 2026. fc_hdd_anom7 is already the forecast version of this anomaly and is not rebuilt here.

Spreads and volatility

S12 = M1 − M2    S13 = M1 − M3    S23 = M2 − M3
S%12 = (M1 − M2) / M2 × 100
S < 0 is contango, S > 0 is backwardation. Model the percent spread. Two euros was noise in August 2022, with TTF near €300, and a real signal with TTF near €20. Returns use the same contract on both days: rt = ln(Ft / Ft−1), then σ = √252 · stdev of the last 20 returns. Expect σ of M1 above M2 above M3. Also store the 20-day standard deviation of ΔS in €/MWh. The z-score and the position size both need it.

Coal-to-gas switch

Coal€/MWh, thermal = API2$/t / EURUSD / 6.978
TTFswitch = (0.52 / 0.38) · (Coal + 0.341 · EUA) − 0.202 · EUA
Switch_gap = TTF M1 − TTFswitch
6.978 MWh per tonne is 6,000 kcal/kg API2 coal. Emission factors are tCO₂ per MWh thermal: gas 0.202, hard coal 0.341. Efficiencies are 52% for a CCGT and 38% for hard coal. Check: API2 at $100/t, EURUSD at 1.08 and EUA at €75 gives a switch near €38/MWh. A positive gap means gas is the more expensive fuel in power, so gas burn falls and the front month should cheapen against M2 and M3. Coal is the filled XAX6 series. Forward-fill only the 44 holiday blanks, with a limit of a few days, before this formula.

05Model

Two models. The level model says where the spread should be, given storage and the other fundamentals. The change model says whether a storage surprise still moves the spread after it is published. Fit the level model first. Add anything else only if it helps out of sample.

Look at the data before fitting

  • S12 and S13 through time, with storage deviation on a second axis.
  • Scatter of S12% against storage_dev, coloured by M1 delivery month. This is the chart in §00, with your points.
  • Correlation of the features. Heating demand, withdrawals and the stock level move together. If two coefficients flip sign, that is collinearity, not a new story.
  • ADF on the spreads and on storage_dev. Engle–Granger between them. A stationary residual is what makes a mean-reversion trade possible.

Model A — fair value

S%12,t = α + β1 Devt + β2 Devt2 + β3 HDD_anom7t + β4 LNG_devt + β5 Switch_gapt + β6 σM1,t + β7 InjUtilt + Σ γm Dm + δ Crisist + εt
OLS with Newey–West standard errors, because daily residuals are autocorrelated: sm.OLS(y, X).fit(cov_type="HAC", cov_kwds={"maxlags": 10}). Same specification for S% of M1/M3. The fitted value is the fair spread. fc_hdd_anom7 is a second specification, added only on the sample from 15 Feb 2024. It does not replace realised HDD_anom7 on the long sample.
VariableSign you should seeReason
Dev, storage above normalβ₁ < 0Extra gas removes the front-month premium. The curve moves toward contango.
Dev²β₂ > 0The curve steepens fast when storage is short, and flattens once it is near the carry floor.
HDD anomaly, colderβ₃ > 0Heating demand hits the front month first, and withdrawals rise.
LNG deviationβ₄ < 0More supply delivered into the front month.
Switch gapβ₅ ≤ 0Gas dearer than coal: power burns less gas, so M1 weakens against M2.
Vol of M1β₆ > 0Tight markets are volatile. The scarcity premium sits in the front month.
Injection utilisationβ₇ < 0Storage cannot take more gas, so the front month has to clear lower.

A wrong sign is a result. Report it. Do not drop the variable to make the table match this one, and do not add lags until the contemporaneous model is written down.

Model B — does the surprise still have a price the next week?

S12, t+h − S12, t = a + b · Surprise7_zt + c · ΔHDD_anom7t + d · zt + ut
h is 1, 5 and 10 trading days. Expect b < 0: more injection than expected cheapens M1 against M2. Expect d < 0: a spread that is already rich tends to fall back. If b is still there at 5 or 10 days, the market is slow, and strategy B has something to do. Both dates must be the same contracts. A change that crosses a roll is not a forecast error.

Residual, z-score, half-life

et = S%12,t − Ŝ%12,t    zt = (et − mean of the last 60 e) / stdev of the last 60 e
et = φ et−1 + ηt  →  half-life = −ln 2 / ln φ
The half-life is how many days a mispricing takes to close by half. It sets the holding period and the time stop. Above roughly 30 days, the signal is slower than the contract you are trading: M1 expires every month.

A curve fit is optional, and it comes after the OLS.

S% = a + b · exp(−c · Dev), with scipy.optimize.curve_fit, is a direct Working curve. A tree model can sit next to it as a benchmark. Keep the linear model as the one you defend: the signs are readable, and ten years of daily data will let a flexible model memorise 2022.

06Where the money is

The instrument is the TTF calendar spread: one month against another, same number of lots. Long the spread means long M1 and short M2. That position gains when S rises, toward backwardation.

A · Fair value

Residual z from Model A
Entry
z > +2, sell the spread. z < −2, buy the spread.
Exit
|z| < 0.5
Stop
|z| > 4, or a hold longer than twice the half-life
Roll
No new trade with fewer than 5 days to expiry. Close or roll at 3.

B · Surprise

Surprise7_z from Model B
Entry
Surprise below −1.5: stocks came out faster than weather explains, buy the spread. Surprise above +1.5: sell the spread.
Hold
A fixed horizon. Use the horizon where Model B still has a coefficient, likely 5 days.
Skip
If the spread already moved more than one standard deviation in that direction over the last 5 days, the surprise is in the price.

C · Both, with a gate

Do not fade a fundamental move
Buy
Only if storage is below normal, or the surprise is negative.
Sell
Only if storage is above normal, or the surprise is positive.
Vol
No new trade when M1 vol is in its top 10% over the past year.

Size and P&L

Same rule for A, B and C
Lot
1 MW for every hour of the delivery month. MWh per lot = 24 × days in the month.
P&L
For a long spread: lots × (MWh of M1 × ΔM1 − MWh of M2 × ΔM2). The two months do not have the same number of days.
Size
Lots = risk budget / (daily stdev of ΔS × MWh per lot).
